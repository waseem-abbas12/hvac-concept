Heating Diagnostics

Common Furnace Breakdown Symptoms We Fix Fast

Pay attention to these clear warnings. Catching component failures early avoids catastrophic heat exchanger cracks and middle-of-the-night shutoffs.

❄️

Blowing Cold or Lukewarm Air

Usually caused by a fouled hot-surface ignitor, dirty flame sensor shutting down the gas valve after 3 seconds, or a tripped high-limit thermal switch due to restricted duct airflow.

⚠️

Yellow or Flickering Burner Flame

CRITICAL SAFETY ALERT: A healthy gas flame must be steady blue. A yellow or dancing flame indicates incomplete combustion and dangerous carbon monoxide production. Call us immediately.

🔊

Booming, Banging, or Squealing

A loud 'boom' at startup is delayed ignition—gas pooling before lighting. Squealing or grinding indicates failing blower motor bearings or a misaligned draft inducer assembly.

🔄

Short Cycling (On & Off Continuously)

When your furnace runs for only 2 to 3 minutes and shuts down, it is overheating. Dirty filters, clogged exhaust flues, or failing draft pressure switches trigger automatic safety lockouts.

Life-Safety Protocol

Why Carbon Monoxide & Heat Exchanger Safety Come First

Your furnace burns natural gas inside a metal combustion chamber called a heat exchanger. Over years of thermal expansion and contraction, micro-fissures can crack open, leaking odorless, poisonous carbon monoxide directly into your home's supply air ductwork.

Every Choice Aire repair includes a non-negotiable combustion safety inspection using high-resolution video fiberoptics and digital flue gas analyzers to guarantee your family's safety.

Emergency Action Checklist Before We Arrive:

  • If your carbon monoxide detector is sounding, evacuate everyone outside immediately and dial 911.
  • Check your thermostat to ensure it is set to HEAT with temperature set 5° above current room temp.
  • Verify your furnace emergency power switch (often looks like a regular light switch with a red cover plate) hasn't been bumped off.
  • Inspect your furnace filter. If it is caked in dust, replace it to relieve high-limit overheating switches.
  • Leave the gas valve and furnace panels untouched until our N.A.T.E. certified technician arrives.
